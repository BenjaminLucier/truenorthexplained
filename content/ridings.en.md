## What is a riding?

A **riding** is the area that elects one member of Parliament (MP) to the House of Commons.
Its official name is a **federal electoral district**. In a federal election, everyone who
lives in a riding chooses among the candidates running there, and the candidate with the
most votes becomes its MP. That's the system called
[first-past-the-post](/learn/elections/first-past-the-post).

Ridings are drawn to hold roughly similar numbers of people within each province, so their
size depends on how many people live nearby. In a big city a riding can be a few
neighbourhoods you could walk across in an hour. In the North a single riding can cover a
whole territory: Nunavut is one riding, about a fifth of Canada's land area. After each
10-year census, an independent commission in each province redraws the boundaries
([how that works](/learn/elections/election-day#ridings-and-their-boundaries)).

> **As of 2026:** There are 343 ridings. Their boundaries were set in 2023 and first used in
> the April 28, 2025 general election.

## The 2025 results, riding by riding

The map shows every riding coloured by the party that won it in 2025. Switch the colours to
see how close each race was (the winner's lead over the second-place candidate) or what
share of registered voters turned out. Cities are too small to see on a map of the whole
country, so use **Zoom to** for a closer look at seven of them.

Select a riding for its full result: every candidate's votes, the margin, turnout, and
whether the seat has changed hands since the election. To find your own riding, search by
name or use **Find my riding**. Your location is matched to a riding inside your browser and
is never sent anywhere.

The MP a riding elected in 2025 isn't always its MP today. A **by-election** fills a seat
when an MP resigns or dies, and an MP can leave their party or join another one between
elections. The election result itself never changes, so the map shows who won in 2025 and
the details note who holds the seat now.
