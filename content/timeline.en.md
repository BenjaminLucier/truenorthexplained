Much of how Canada works today makes sense only once you know how it got that way. This
timeline gathers the dates mentioned across the site's pages, grouped into eras. Each
entry links to the page that explains why it matters.

<!-- visual: horizontal interactive timeline filterable by module -->

## Before Confederation (to 1866)

- **1701** — The Crown begins signing the historic treaties with Indigenous nations, a process that runs until 1923. [Read more](/learn/indigenous/treaties)
- **1758** — Nova Scotia's House of Assembly first meets, on October 2, becoming the oldest elected legislature in what is now Canada. [Read more](/provinces/nova-scotia)
- **1763** — The Royal Proclamation (October 7) says only the Crown can acquire Indigenous lands, with the nation's agreement, laying the root of the treaty process. [Read more](/learn/indigenous/trea [... omitted end of long line]
- **1764** — The Crown and many Indigenous nations meet at Niagara; many see this Treaty of Niagara as accepting the Proclamation's promises nation to nation. [Read more](/learn/indigenous/treaties) [... omitted end of long line]
- **1774** — The Quebec Act restores French civil law for property and civil rights, which is why Quebec uses civil law today. [Read more](/learn/law/common-law-and-civil-law) · [Quebec](/provinces/qu [... omitted end of long line]
- **1784** — New Brunswick is split off from Nova Scotia as a separate colony, largely for Loyalist settlers. [Read more](/provinces/new-brunswick)
- **1791** — The colony of Upper Canada, today's Ontario, is created. [Read more](/provinces/ontario)
- **1841** — Upper and Lower Canada are joined into the Province of Canada. [Read more](/provinces/ontario)
- **1848** — In February, Nova Scotia becomes the first colony in the British Empire with responsible government; the Province of Canada follows weeks later. [Read more](/provinces/nova-scotia) · [Con [... omitted end of long line]
- **1855** — Newfoundland wins responsible government but stays outside the later Confederation. [Read more](/provinces/newfoundland-and-labrador)
- **1864** — In September, the Charlottetown Conference becomes the first of the meetings that lead to Confederation. [Read more](/provinces/prince-edward-island)
- **1866** — The Civil Code of Lower Canada comes into force. [Read more](/learn/law/common-law-and-civil-law)

## Building the country (1867–1923)

- **1867** — On July 1, the Constitution Act, 1867 (then the BNA Act) unites Ontario, Quebec, Nova Scotia and New Brunswick and divides powers between Ottawa and the provinces. [Read more](/learn/cons [... omitted end of long line]
- **1869** — Canada arranges to take over Rupert's Land; the Métis at Red River, led by Louis Riel, form a provisional government (the Red River Resistance). [Read more](/provinces/manitoba)
- **1870** — Canada acquires Rupert's Land and the North-Western Territory, which become the North-West Territories. [Read more](/learn/federalism/territories) · [Northwest Territories](/provinces/nor [... omitted end of long line]
- **1870** — The Manitoba Act makes Manitoba a province on July 15, with English and French built into its founding law. [Read more](/provinces/manitoba)
- **1871** — British Columbia joins Canada, on the promise of a railway link to the rest of the country. [Read more](/provinces/british-columbia)
- **1871** — Treaty 1 is signed, the first of the Numbered Treaties that run to 1921 across the West and North. [Read more](/learn/indigenous/treaties)
- **1873** — Prince Edward Island joins Canada, six years after the first four provinces. [Read more](/provinces/prince-edward-island)
- **1875** — Parliament creates the Supreme Court of Canada. [Read more](/learn/law/supreme-court)
- **1876** — Parliament passes the Indian Act, which still defines status and governs reserves today. [Read more](/learn/indigenous/indian-act)
- **1884** — The Indian Act is changed to ban ceremonies such as the potlatch; the ban lasts until 1951. [Read more](/learn/indigenous/indian-act)
- **1885** — Louis Riel is executed for treason after the North-West Resistance. [Read more](/provinces/manitoba)
- **1892** — Canada's first Criminal Code is passed. [Read more](/learn/law/criminal-law)
- **1898** — Yukon becomes a separate territory during the Klondike Gold Rush. [Read more](/provinces/yukon) · [Territories](/learn/federalism/territories)
- **1905** — Parliament creates Alberta and Saskatchewan out of the North-West Territories. [Read more](/provinces/alberta) · [Saskatchewan](/provinces/saskatchewan)
- **1920** — Residential school attendance becomes mandatory for many First Nations children. [Read more](/learn/indigenous/indian-act)
- **1923** — The Williams Treaties, the last of the historic treaties, are signed in southern Ontario. [Read more](/learn/indigenous/treaties)

## Becoming fully independent (1926–1981)

- **1926** — In the King–Byng affair, the Governor General refuses the Prime Minister's request for an election, the last time this happened federally. [Read more](/learn/crown/reserve-powers) · [Conv [... omitted end of long line]
- **1926** — The Imperial Conference establishes that a Governor General represents the monarch personally, not the British government. [Read more](/learn/crown/reserve-powers)
- **1929** — In the Persons Case, the Privy Council rules women are "persons" who can sit in the Senate and calls the Constitution "a living tree". [Read more](/learn/constitution/constitution-act-186 [... omitted end of long line]
- **1930** — The Natural Resources Transfer Agreements finally give Manitoba, Saskatchewan and Alberta control of their land and resources. [Read more](/provinces/alberta) · [Saskatchewan](/provinces/ [... omitted end of long line]
- **1931** — The Statute of Westminster ends Britain's power to make laws for Canada without its consent, though it leaves the BNA Acts out. [Read more](/learn/constitution/patriation-1982) · [The Constitution](/learn/constitution)
- **1933** — Criminal appeals from Canada to the Privy Council in London end. [Read more](/learn/law/supreme-court)
- **1934** — Newfoundland, near bankruptcy, has its legislature suspended and is run by an appointed Commission of Government. [Read more](/provinces/newfoundland-and-labrador)
- **1935** — The Bank of Canada begins operating; it becomes publicly owned in 1938. [Read more](/learn/government/bank-of-canada-vs-mint)
- **1939** — A Supreme Court reference decides that Inuit are "Indians" under federal power in s. 91(24). [Read more](/learn/indigenous/section-35)
- **1947** — Saskatchewan launches universal hospital insurance and passes one of Canada's first bills of rights. [Read more](/provinces/saskatchewan)
- **1948** — In two referendums (June 3 and July 22), Newfoundlanders narrowly choose to join Canada. [Read more](/provinces/newfoundland-and-labrador)
- **1949** — Newfoundland joins Canada on March 31, the most recent province to do so. [Read more](/provinces/newfoundland-and-labrador)
- **1949** — All remaining appeals to the Privy Council end, making the Supreme Court Canada's final court. [Read more](/learn/law/supreme-court)
- **1951** — Major Indian Act changes remove the ban on ceremonies and the limit on hiring lawyers for land claims. [Read more](/learn/indigenous/indian-act)
- **1957** — The federal equalization program begins. [Read more](/learn/federalism/money-and-equalization)
- **1960** — Parliament passes the Canadian Bill of Rights, an ordinary federal law that comes before the Charter. [Read more](/learn/charter) · [1867 Act](/learn/constitution/constitution-act-1867) [... omitted end of long line]
- **1960** — First Nations people gain the right to vote federally without losing status. [Read more](/learn/indigenous/indian-act)
- **1961** — Saskatchewan's Lieutenant Governor reserves a bill for Ottawa, the last use of the reservation power. [Read more](/learn/crown/reserve-powers)
- **1962** — Saskatchewan brings in universal medical care insurance despite a doctors' strike. [Read more](/provinces/saskatchewan)
- **1965** — Senators must now retire at 75 instead of serving for life. [Read more](/learn/parliament/senate) · [1867 Act](/learn/constitution/constitution-act-1867)
- **1966** — Parliament passes the Medical Care Act, offering to share the cost of provincial medicare plans. [Read more](/provinces/saskatchewan) · [Money](/learn/federalism/money-and-equalization) [... omitted end of long line]
- **1968** — Quebec renames its legislature the National Assembly and abolishes its Legislative Council, the last province to have one. [Read more](/provinces/quebec)
- **1969** — A federal White Paper proposes abolishing the Indian Act and status; First Nations reject it and it is withdrawn. [Read more](/learn/indigenous/indian-act)
- **1973** — The *Calder* decision recognizes that Aboriginal title exists in Canadian law, prompting modern treaty talks. [Read more](/learn/indigenous/section-35) · [Treaties](/learn/indigenous/trea [... omitted end of long line]
- **1975** — The James Bay and Northern Quebec Agreement becomes the first modern treaty. [Read more](/learn/indigenous/treaties)
- **1975** — Quebec adopts its own Charter of Human Rights and Freedoms. [Read more](/provinces/quebec)
- **1977** — Quebec passes the Charter of the French Language (Bill 101). [Read more](/provinces/quebec)
- **1980** — Quebecers vote No, by about 60%, to a mandate to negotiate sovereignty-association. [Read more](/provinces/quebec) · [Patriation](/learn/constitution/patriation-1982)
- **1981** — In the Patriation Reference (September), the Supreme Court says unilateral patriation would be legal but break a constitutional convention. [Read more](/learn/constitution/patriation-1982 [... omitted end of long line]
- **1981** — In November, Ottawa and nine provinces strike a patriation deal without Quebec, including the notwithstanding clause. [Read more](/learn/constitution/patriation-1982) · [Section 33](/lear [... omitted end of long line]

## The Charter era (1982–1999)

- **1982** — On April 17, the Constitution Act, 1982 comes into force, adding the Charter, an amending formula and section 35, and ending Britain's last formal role. [Read more](/learn/constitution/pa [... omitted end of long line]
- **1982** — Section 92A is added to the Constitution, confirming provincial power over natural resources. [Read more](/provinces/alberta) · [Division of powers](/learn/federalism/division-of-powers) [... omitted end of long line]
- **1982** — In the Quebec Veto Reference (December), the Supreme Court rules Quebec had no veto over patriation. [Read more](/learn/constitution/patriation-1982)
- **1983** — Section 35 is amended to protect land claims agreements and guarantee Aboriginal rights equally to men and women. [Read more](/learn/indigenous/section-35) · [Amending formula](/learn/con [... omitted end of long line]
- **1985** — Section 15, the Charter's equality rights, comes into force on April 17. [Read more](/learn/charter/your-rights)
- **1985** — Bill C-31 ends the Indian Act rule that took status from women who married non-status men. [Read more](/learn/indigenous/indian-act)
- **1985** — The Supreme Court rules Manitoba's English-only laws invalid and gives the province time to re-enact them in both languages. [Read more](/provinces/manitoba)
- **1986** — *R. v. Oakes* creates the test for deciding whether a limit on a Charter right is reasonable. [Read more](/learn/charter/reasonable-limits) · [Landmark cases](/learn/charter/landmark-case [... omitted end of long line]
- **1987** — The federal government and all ten premiers agree to the Meech Lake Accord. [Read more](/learn/constitution/patriation-1982)
- **1988** — *R. v. Morgentaler* strikes down the Criminal Code's abortion provisions. [Read more](/learn/charter/landmark-cases)
- **1988** — *Ford v. Quebec* protects commercial signs as expression and sets the rules for using section 33; Quebec then uses it. [Read more](/learn/charter/notwithstanding-clause) · [Landmark cases [... omitted end of long line]
- **1990** — The Meech Lake Accord dies when its June 23 ratification deadline passes. [Read more](/learn/constitution/patriation-1982) · [Quebec](/provinces/quebec)
- **1990** — *Sparrow*, the first major section 35 case, says Aboriginal rights can be limited only with justification. [Read more](/learn/indigenous/section-35)
- **1992** — Voters reject the Charlottetown Accord in a national referendum on October 26. [Read more](/learn/constitution/patriation-1982) · [Self-government](/learn/indigenous/self-government)
- **1993** — The Nunavut Land Claims Agreement is signed, and Parliament passes the Nunavut Act. [Read more](/provinces/nunavut) · [Treaties](/learn/indigenous/treaties)
- **1993** — A bilateral amendment adds the equality of New Brunswick's English and French communities to the Charter. [Read more](/provinces/new-brunswick) · [Amending formula](/learn/constitution/am [... omitted end of long line]
- **1994** — The Civil Code of Québec comes into force on January 1. [Read more](/learn/law/common-law-and-civil-law) · [Quebec](/provinces/quebec)
- **1995** — In a second referendum, Quebecers vote No to sovereignty by about 50.6% to 49.4%. [Read more](/provinces/quebec) · [Patriation](/learn/constitution/patriation-1982)
- **1995** — The federal government recognizes the inherent right of self-government as a section 35 right. [Read more](/learn/indigenous/self-government)
- **1996** — Parliament passes the "regional veto" law on constitutional amendments. [Read more](/learn/constitution/amending-formula)
- **1997** — *Delgamuukw* explains what Aboriginal title is and accepts oral histories as evidence. [Read more](/learn/indigenous/section-35)
- **1998** — In the Secession Reference, the Supreme Court says Quebec can't leave unilaterally, but a clear vote would require negotiation. [Read more](/learn/law/supreme-court) · [Quebec](/provinces [... omitted end of long line]
- **1998** — *Vriend v. Alberta* reads sexual orientation into Alberta's human rights law. [Read more](/learn/charter/landmark-cases)
- **1999** — Nunavut is created on April 1 from the eastern part of the Northwest Territories. [Read more](/provinces/nunavut) · [Territories](/learn/federalism/territories)
- **1999** — *R. v. Marshall* holds that 1760–61 treaties protect a Mi'kmaq right to fish and trade for a moderate livelihood. [Read more](/learn/indigenous/treaties) · [Nova Scotia](/provinces/nova-s [... omitted end of long line]

## Recent (2000–today)

- **2000** — Parliament passes the Clarity Act in response to the Secession Reference. [Read more](/provinces/quebec)
- **2000** — The Nisga'a Final Agreement, BC's first modern treaty, takes effect. [Read more](/learn/indigenous/treaties) · [British Columbia](/provinces/british-columbia)
- **2001** — A constitutional amendment renames Newfoundland as Newfoundland and Labrador. [Read more](/provinces/newfoundland-and-labrador)
- **2003** — On April 1, Yukon becomes the first territory to manage its own public lands and resources. [Read more](/provinces/yukon) · [Territories](/learn/federalism/territories)
- **2003** — The premiers create the Council of the Federation. [Read more](/learn/federalism)
- **2004** — *Haida Nation* establishes the Crown's duty to consult. [Read more](/learn/indigenous/section-35)
- **2004** — In the Same-Sex Marriage Reference, the Supreme Court says Parliament can define marriage to include same-sex couples. [Read more](/learn/law/supreme-court)
- **2005** — The Labrador Inuit Land Claims Agreement takes effect, creating the Nunatsiavut Government. [Read more](/provinces/newfoundland-and-labrador) · [Self-government](/learn/indigenous/self-go [... omitted end of long line]
- **2006** — The House of Commons recognizes that the Québécois form a nation within a united Canada. [Read more](/provinces/quebec)
- **2007** — Parliament sets fixed federal election dates, which don't stop early elections. [Read more](/learn/elections/election-day) · [Elections](/learn/elections)
- **2008** — The federal government apologizes for residential schools, and the Truth and Reconciliation Commission is created. [Read more](/learn/indigenous/indian-act)
- **2008** — On December 4, Parliament is prorogued before an expected non-confidence vote. [Read more](/learn/crown/reserve-powers) · [Confidence](/learn/parliament/confidence)
- **2013** — Parliament passes the Succession to the Throne Act. [Read more](/learn/crown/republic-question)
- **2014** — The Senate Reform Reference rules Senate elections or term limits need provincial consent. [Read more](/learn/parliament/senate) · [Amending formula](/learn/constitution/amending-formula) [... omitted end of long line]
- **2014** — *Tsilhqot'in* becomes the first court declaration of Aboriginal title to a specific area. [Read more](/learn/indigenous/section-35) · [British Columbia](/provinces/british-columbia)
- **2014** — On April 1, the Northwest Territories takes over its public lands and resources. [Read more](/provinces/northwest-territories)
- **2015** — The Truth and Reconciliation Commission publishes its findings and 94 Calls to Action. [Read more](/learn/indigenous/indian-act) · [Indigenous peoples](/learn/indigenous)
- **2015** — *Carter v. Canada* strikes down the total ban on assisted dying. [Read more](/learn/charter/landmark-cases)
- **2016** — *Daniels* rules that Métis and non-status Indians fall under federal power in s. 91(24). [Read more](/learn/indigenous/section-35)
- **2016** — *R. v. Jordan* sets time limits for criminal trials. [Read more](/learn/charter/landmark-cases)
- **2016** — Parliament passes a law on medical assistance in dying. [Read more](/learn/charter/landmark-cases)
- **2016** — An Independent Advisory Board begins recommending Senate appointments. [Read more](/learn/parliament/senate)
- **2017** — The federal government drops its plan to replace first-past-the-post. [Read more](/learn/elections/first-past-the-post)
- **2019** — British Columbia passes DRIPA, the first Canadian law committing a government to align its laws with UNDRIP. [Read more](/provinces/british-columbia)
- **2019** — Parliament affirms Indigenous jurisdiction over child and family services. [Read more](/learn/indigenous/self-government)
- **2019** — Quebec uses the notwithstanding clause in its state secularism law. [Read more](/learn/charter/notwithstanding-clause)
- **2021** — Parliament passes the United Nations Declaration on the Rights of Indigenous Peoples Act. [Read more](/learn/indigenous/self-government) · [Indigenous peoples](/learn/indigenous)
- **2021** — The Supreme Court upholds federal minimum carbon pricing under the "national concern" branch of POGG. [Read more](/learn/federalism/division-of-powers)
- **2021** — The Supreme Court upholds Ontario's mid-election cut to Toronto's city council. [Read more](/learn/federalism/municipalities) · [Ontario](/provinces/ontario)
- **2021** — Alberta holds a non-binding referendum in which most voters back removing equalization from the Constitution. [Read more](/learn/federalism/money-and-equalization)
- **2022** — Charles III becomes King of Canada in September. [Read more](/learn/crown)
- **2022** — Quebec adds sections to the 1867 Act on its own and expands its French-language law (Bill 96). [Read more](/learn/constitution/amending-formula) · [Quebec](/provinces/quebec)
- **2024** — Nunavut signs its lands and resources devolution agreement on January 18. [Read more](/provinces/nunavut)
- **2024** — The Supreme Court upholds the federal law on Indigenous child and family services. [Read more](/learn/indigenous/self-government)
- **2025** — Alberta uses the notwithstanding clause in a law ending a teachers' strike. [Read more](/learn/charter/notwithstanding-clause)

> **As of 2026:** Nunavut's transfer of lands and resources is scheduled for April 1,
> 2027. See [Nunavut](/provinces/nunavut).
