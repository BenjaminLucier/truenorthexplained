Canadians have voted in {{elections.count}} federal general elections since Confederation. Each one fills
every seat in the [House of Commons](org:house-of-commons), and whoever can hold the House's
[confidence](/learn/parliament/confidence) forms the government. Hover over or tap any
chart, or tab to it and use the arrow keys, to see each election's numbers. Every chart
also has its data as a table.

## Who won the seats

<tn-widget name="election-seats"></tn-widget>

Parties have come and gone, so the charts group them into **families**. The Conservative
family is the Conservative, Liberal-Conservative and Unionist parties, then the
Progressive Conservatives, and from 1993 to 2003 also Reform and the Canadian Alliance,
which merged with the Progressive Conservatives to form today's Conservative Party. CCF /
NDP is the Co-operative Commonwealth Federation and the New Democratic Party that
replaced it in 1961. The Progressive family is the farmers' parties of the 1920s and the
Liberal-Progressives who sat with the Liberals. Social Credit includes the Ralliement
créditiste. Independents, Labour members and short-lived parties are under Other.

## How votes become seats

Under [first-past-the-post](/learn/elections/first-past-the-post), the candidate with the
most votes in each [riding](term:riding) wins it, and the rest of the votes there elect no one. Across
the country, that tends to give the biggest party more seats than its share of the vote.

## Who votes

Turnout is the share of registered electors who cast a ballot. Who was allowed to
register has changed a great deal: the numbered milestones mark the biggest changes.

## Majority and minority

A party with more than half the seats can pass its bills and survive
[confidence votes](/learn/parliament/confidence) on its own. A
[minority government](term:minority-government) needs support from other parties,
and those Parliaments usually end sooner.

<tn-widget name="election-governments"></tn-widget>

## A bigger House

Seats are redistributed among the provinces after each census, and the House grows as
the population does. Provinces joining Confederation added seats too: Manitoba,
British Columbia and Prince Edward Island in the 1870s, and Newfoundland in 1949.

<tn-widget name="election-house-size"></tn-widget>

## Women in the House

Women got the federal vote on the same terms as men in time for the 1921 election, when
[Agnes Macphail](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Agnes_Macphail) became the first woman
elected to the House.
